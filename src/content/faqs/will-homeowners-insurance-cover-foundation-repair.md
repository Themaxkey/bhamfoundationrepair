Usually not, and the reason is worth understanding before you call your agent, because it decides how you should approach the whole thing.

## The General Rule

Standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental damage. They exclude gradual damage, and they specifically exclude earth movement — soil that shifts, swells, shrinks or settles under a house.

That exclusion covers the overwhelming majority of foundation trouble in Jefferson County. Red clay expanding after a wet spring and contracting through a dry August is not sudden and it is not accidental. It is the ground doing exactly what that soil has always done. An insurer looks at a stair-step crack in brick and sees years of seasonal movement, which is precisely what the policy is written to exclude.

Poor drainage, deferred maintenance and original construction defects are excluded on the same logic. A house built on poorly compacted fill was, from the insurer's point of view, always going to do this.

## What Usually Is Covered

The exception is damage traceable to a specific covered event. Depending on your policy, that can include a burst or leaking supply line beneath the slab, a tree coming down on the structure, vehicle impact, fire, explosion, lightning, and wind or tornado damage.

The plumbing one matters most here, and it is the case people miss. A supply line failing under a slab can wash out or soften the soil beneath a footing and produce settlement that looks identical to ordinary clay movement. Same cracks, same sloping floor, completely different cause — and a cause that is sudden and accidental rather than gradual.

Even then, coverage is often partial. Many policies will pay to access the leak and repair the resulting water damage while declining the foundation work itself. Some pay for both. The wording varies more between policies than most people expect.

## Why the Cause Comes First

This is the practical point. If you claim before anyone has established why the foundation moved, you are asking an adjuster to categorize the damage with no evidence, and the default category is the excluded one.

If there is any suspicion of a plumbing leak — an unexplained rise in the water bill, a warm patch on a slab floor, damp that appears in one spot rather than along a wall — get a static pressure test or a leak isolation test done first. A plumber's written report identifying a failed line under the slab is the single piece of evidence that moves a claim from excluded to arguable.

Photographs help too, especially dated ones showing a crack that was not there last year. Sudden matters.

## Earthquake and Flood

Both are excluded from standard policies and both are separate products. Alabama sits far enough from major fault activity that earthquake cover is rarely worth it, but flood is a different conversation depending on where in the metro you are, and flooding is a genuine cause of foundation and crawl space damage.

## What to Do

Read your own declarations page rather than relying on general guidance, including this page. Policies genuinely differ, and the exclusions section is where the answer lives.

Then get the cause diagnosed before you decide whether to claim at all. A denied claim on your record is not free, and if the cause turns out to be ordinary clay movement you are better off knowing that and planning the repair than opening a file that was always going to be closed.
