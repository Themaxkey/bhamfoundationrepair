Encapsulation seals a crawl space from the earth below and the outside air, turning it into a dry space that behaves like part of the house. In central Alabama it solves a real problem — but not every crawl space has that problem, and you should know which you have before you spend.

## Why Vented Crawl Spaces Fail Here

Codes required vents for decades on the theory that outside air dries the space out. In a humid climate it does the reverse.

Birmingham summers sit above seventy percent relative humidity for long stretches. That warm wet air comes through the vents and meets framing that is cooler, and the moisture condenses onto joists, ducts and insulation. You are pumping water under the house all summer.

Bare earth adds to it continuously, releasing vapor upward day and night.

## What That Does

Wood above roughly twenty percent moisture content becomes hospitable to decay fungi, and joists, girders and subfloor start to soften. Springy floors upstairs are often a moisture problem underneath rather than a framing defect.

Fiberglass batts absorb water, lose most of their insulating value, get heavy and fall out of the joist bays. That is the insulation hanging down in half the crawl spaces in Jefferson County.

Air moves upward through a house, so a meaningful share of what you breathe on the ground floor has come from the crawl space. Musty smells in the living room usually start below it.

## What Encapsulation Involves

A heavy reinforced liner across the floor and up the walls, sealed at seams and perimeter. Vents sealed. The rim joist sealed, which is often the largest single air leak in an older house. Any liquid water given a drainage route and a sump before anything is covered. A dedicated dehumidifier to hold the humidity down. Insulation moved to the foundation walls rather than between the floor joists.

## When You Do Not Need It

If your crawl space is dry, has an intact vapor barrier over the earth, shows no standing water, and wood moisture readings come back in a normal range, you may not need encapsulation at all. Improving the drainage outside and replacing a torn barrier can be enough.

Anyone who inspects a dry crawl space and still recommends a full encapsulation package should be asked to show you the moisture readings that justify it.
