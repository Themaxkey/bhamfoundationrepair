Hoover is the largest suburb in the Birmingham metro, spread across Jefferson and Shelby Counties on the far side of Shades Mountain, and its foundation problems are shaped almost entirely by how and when it was built.

## What Hoover Homes Are Built On

Most of Hoover went up between the 1970s and the 2000s, in subdivisions carved into sloping wooded ground. That matters more than the age of the houses. Building a level street and level house pads on a hillside means cutting into the slope on the uphill side and filling on the downhill side, and a house whose footprint straddles that boundary sits half on undisturbed ground and half on engineered fill.

Where the fill was properly compacted and given time, this is fine for decades. Where it was rushed, or where the fill was placed over a stump-filled clearing, the filled side consolidates and the house comes with it. That is why settlement in Hoover so often shows up on one specific corner rather than around the whole perimeter.

Bluff Park is the exception worth noting. Its older cottages along the brow of the mountain date from well before the subdivision era and sit on shallower foundations over rock, with a different set of characteristics again.

## What We See Here

Slab-on-grade construction dominates the newer neighborhoods — Trace Crossings, Ross Bridge, Lake Cyrus and the developments around them. Slab problems announce themselves through diagonal cracking above doors and windows, floors that have developed a noticeable fall across a room, and cracks in tile that run in a consistent line rather than randomly.

Walk-out basements and daylight basements are common where the lot falls away sharply. Those below-grade walls take lateral pressure from saturated clay uphill of them, and horizontal cracking or inward bowing in a basement wall is a different problem from settlement, needing anchors or carbon fiber rather than piers.

Crawl spaces in the older sections show the usual Alabama pattern of humidity, failed vapor barriers and sagging girders.

## Drainage Is Usually Involved

On sloped Hoover lots, water moves through the soil downhill and arrives at whichever wall is uphill. Before anyone quotes you structural work, it is worth checking the simple things: where downspouts discharge, whether the grade genuinely falls away from the house for the first several feet, and whether a patio or driveway has settled so that it now drains back toward the foundation.

Fixing those is inexpensive next to piering, and on a meaningful share of Hoover houses it addresses the cause rather than the symptom.

## Getting a Straight Answer

Photographs help a great deal — the affected wall inside and out, a close-up of any crack with a coin or tape for scale, and the crawl space or basement if you can safely get to it. It is often possible to tell from those whether you are looking at active settlement, lateral pressure, or curing cracks that need nothing at all.
