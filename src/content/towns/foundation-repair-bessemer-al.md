Bessemer was founded as an iron and steel town in the 1880s and grew fast, and a good deal of its housing dates from the first half of the twentieth century. That age is the dominant factor in the foundation work needed here, and it makes Bessemer a very different proposition from the newer suburbs south of the city.

## Older Construction, Shallower Footings

Houses built in the 1900s to 1940s were generally founded on shallow footings, or in many cases on masonry piers with no proper footing at all — brick or stone set more or less directly on the clay. Standards were different, and expectations of what a foundation had to do were different.

A hundred years of Alabama wet-dry cycling under a shallow footing produces settlement. It tends to be gradual and long-running rather than sudden, which is why so many older Bessemer homes have visible historic movement that has been stable for decades alongside newer cracking that has not.

Distinguishing old movement from active movement is the single most useful thing an inspection does here. A crack that opened in 1970 and has not changed since needs nothing. The same crack widening this year needs attention.

## Crawl Spaces and What Is Under Them

Most of the older stock is pier-and-beam over a vented crawl space. Expect dry-stacked masonry piers, timber shims that have rotted or crushed, girders sagging between supports, and floors that slope toward the middle of the house.

The repair is usually rebuilding interior support properly: new footings sized for the load, adjustable steel jacks or new piers on top of them, steel shims rather than wood, and sistering or replacing joists and girders where the timber has softened. It is a considerably cheaper job than perimeter underpinning and it is what many of these houses actually need.

Termite damage is common in this housing stock and should be identified before rebuilding on top of it.

## Ground Conditions

Western Jefferson County has a long industrial and mining history, and questions about ground stability come up locally. Where there is any reason to suspect an issue beyond ordinary clay movement, that is a matter for a licensed geotechnical or structural engineer rather than a repair contractor, and any contractor who dismisses the question without one is overreaching.

For the overwhelming majority of Bessemer houses, though, what is happening is straightforward: shallow footings on expansive clay, plus a century of drainage that was never quite right.

## Value for Money

Bessemer property values mean it is entirely possible to be quoted structural work costing a significant fraction of what the house is worth. A contractor worth dealing with will tell you plainly where the boundary sits between repairs that protect the house and repairs that cost more than the problem. Ask that question directly.
