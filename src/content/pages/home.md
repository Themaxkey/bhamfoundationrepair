## Foundation Repair in Birmingham, AL

Birmingham sits on red clay with a high shrink-swell potential, and that single fact explains most of the foundation trouble in this metro. The clay expands when it takes on water and contracts when it dries, and with roughly fifty-four inches of rain a year arriving in bursts between hot dry spells, the ground under your house changes volume every season.

Add the terrain — a ridge-and-valley city where a great many homes sit on cut-and-fill lots straddling undisturbed ground and uncompacted fill — and you have the two causes behind the overwhelming majority of local settlement.

## Why Houses Move Around Jefferson County

Clay shrinkage during drought pulls soil away from footings and leaves them spanning a void. Poorly compacted fill on hillside lots consolidates for years after construction. Water discharging in the wrong place — a downspout at a corner, a driveway pitched back toward the slab — softens one patch of soil and not the one beside it.

Older neighborhoods like Homewood and Mountain Brook add a further factor: ninety years of humid air circulating through vented crawl spaces, softening the girders and joists that hold the floors up.

## What Happens When You Call

We look at the outside first, because a surprising share of what gets diagnosed as structural failure turns out to be drainage that has been running wrong for years, and that is far cheaper to fix.

Then inside, checking floors for slope, doors and windows for racking, and the pattern of any cracking — because a diagonal crack from the corner of a door means something quite different from a horizontal crack across a block wall, and they need different repairs.

Then underneath, where there is a crawl space or basement, since that is where the evidence usually is.

You get a plain description of what is happening, whether it is active, and what the options are. If the honest answer is that your house does not need structural work, that is what you will be told.

## Send Photographs

Photographs answer a lot of questions before anyone visits. A wide shot of the affected wall inside and out, a close-up of any crack with a coin or tape measure for scale, and a picture of the crawl space if you can safely reach it will often be enough to say whether you are looking at active settlement, lateral soil pressure, or ordinary curing cracks that need nothing at all.
